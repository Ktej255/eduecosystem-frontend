अर्जुन विषाद योग

Chapter 1: Arjuna's Distress

Arjuna Vishada Yoga — The Yoga of Arjuna's Grief | 47 Verses

Overview

The first chapter of the Bhagavad Gita sets the stage for the entire discourse. It opens on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, where two great armies—the Pandavas and the Kauravas—are poised for war. King Dhritarashtra, who is blind, asks his minister Sanjaya to describe the events on the battlefield.

As the conch shells blow and the war is about to commence, Prince Arjuna asks his charioteer Lord Krishna to drive his chariot between the two armies. There, seeing his own kinsmen—grandfathers, teachers, uncles, brothers, sons, grandsons, and friends—arranged on both sides, Arjuna is overcome with grief and compassion.

Key Themes

😢 Moral Dilemma

Arjuna faces the ultimate ethical conflict: fighting for righteousness means killing his own family.

🏹 Duty vs. Emotion

The tension between Arjuna's duty as a warrior and his love for his kinsmen creates inner turmoil.

🤔 The Need for Guidance

Arjuna's confusion sets the stage for Krishna's divine teachings in subsequent chapters.

Arjuna's Lament

"O Krishna, seeing my kinsmen gathered here, eager to fight, my limbs fail and my mouth is parched. My body trembles, and my hair stands on end." (1.28-29)

"What pleasure can we derive from killing the sons of Dhritarashtra? Sin alone will accrue to us from killing these aggressors." (1.36)

"It would be better for me if the sons of Dhritarashtra, weapons in hand, were to kill me unarmed and unresisting on the battlefield." (1.46)

Summary

Chapter 1 concludes with Arjuna casting aside his bow and arrows and sinking down on the seat of his chariot, his mind overwhelmed with grief. He declares that he will not fight.

This chapter is crucial because it presents the human problem—doubt, attachment, and moral confusion—that the rest of the Gita will address. Without this crisis, there would be no teaching. Arjuna's vulnerability opens the door for divine wisdom.
