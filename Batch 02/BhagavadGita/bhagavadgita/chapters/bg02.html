सांख्य योग

Chapter 2: Samkhya Yoga

The Yoga of Knowledge | 72 Verses

Overview

Chapter 2 is considered the essence of the entire Gita. After Arjuna's complete surrender in Chapter 1, Lord Krishna begins His teachings. He starts by addressing Arjuna's grief and then reveals the profound truth about the nature of the Self (Atman).

This chapter introduces the foundational concepts of Vedantic philosophy: the eternal, indestructible soul; the temporary nature of the body; and the importance of performing one's duty without attachment to results. It lays the groundwork for all the teachings that follow.

Key Themes

🕉️ The Eternal Soul

The Atman is never born and never dies. It is eternal, unchanging, and indestructible.

⚖️ Karma Yoga Intro

Perform your duty without attachment to the fruits of action - this is the key to liberation.

🧘 Sthitaprajna

The characteristics of a person of steady wisdom who has transcended the pairs of opposites.

Most Famous Verses

2.20: "The soul is neither born, nor does it ever die. Having come into being once, it never ceases to be. It is unborn, eternal, permanent, and primeval."

2.22: "As a person puts on new garments, giving up old ones, the soul similarly accepts new material bodies, giving up the old and useless ones."

2.47: "You have the right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself the cause of the results, and never be attached to not doing your duty."

2.48: "Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga."

Summary

Chapter 2 establishes the philosophical foundation of the Gita. Krishna teaches that the true Self is immortal, and therefore Arjuna need not grieve for the bodies that will be destroyed. He introduces the concept of Nishkama Karma (selfless action) and describes the qualities of a Sthitaprajna - one whose mind is steady in wisdom.

This chapter is often called the "Summary Chapter" because it contains the seeds of all the major teachings expanded in later chapters.
