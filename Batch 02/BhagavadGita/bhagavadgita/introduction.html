Introduction to Bhagavad Gita

The Song of God — A dialogue between Lord Krishna and Arjuna

What is the Bhagavad Gita?

The Bhagavad Gita (Sanskrit: भगवद्गीता, "The Song of God") is a 700-verse Hindu scripture that is part of the Indian epic Mahabharata. It is a sacred dialogue between Prince Arjuna and Lord Krishna, who serves as his charioteer.

Set on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, just before a great war is about to begin, Arjuna is overcome with moral doubt and despair about fighting his own relatives. Lord Krishna, revealing himself as the Supreme Being, imparts profound spiritual wisdom to guide Arjuna—and through him, all of humanity—toward righteousness, duty, and liberation.

The Three Paths to Liberation

🧘 Jnana Yoga

The Path of Knowledge — Liberation through discrimination between the real and the unreal, understanding the nature of the Self (Atman) and Brahman.

🙏 Bhakti Yoga

The Path of Devotion — Liberation through loving surrender to God, cultivating devotion, and seeing the Divine in all beings.

⚡ Karma Yoga

The Path of Action — Liberation through selfless action, performing one's duty without attachment to results.

Key Teachings

On the Eternal Soul: "The soul is neither born, nor does it ever die. It is unborn, eternal, permanent, and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain." (2.20)

On Duty: "You have the right to perform your prescribed duties, but you are not entitled to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself to be the cause of the results, nor be attached to inaction." (2.47)

On Equanimity: "One who is equal in pleasure and pain, self-reliant, to whom a clod of earth, a stone, and gold are the same, the wise and the unwise are alike, and who is unaffected by praise or blame—such one is dear to Me." (14.24-25)

The 18 Chapters Overview

The Gita is divided into 18 chapters, each called a "Yoga" (union/path). They can be grouped into three sections:

Chapters 1-6: Karma Yoga Section

Focus on action, duty, and the individual soul. Arjuna's dilemma, the nature of the Self, and the importance of selfless action.

Chapters 7-12: Bhakti Yoga Section

Focus on devotion, knowledge of the Divine, and the cosmic vision. Krishna reveals His divine nature and the power of devotion.

Chapters 13-18: Jnana Yoga Section

Focus on knowledge, the three gunas, the supreme person, and final liberation. The synthesis of all teachings and Arjuna's enlightenment.

Why Study the Gita?

For thousands of years, the Bhagavad Gita has been a source of guidance for millions. It addresses the eternal questions:

  • ✨ Who am I? What is my true nature?
  • 🎯 What is my purpose and duty in life?
  • 🕉️ How can I find inner peace amidst life's challenges?
  • 🌟 How can I attain liberation (Moksha)?
  • 💫 How should I live and act in this world?
