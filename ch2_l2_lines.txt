[0369]: Q1. James Mill, a prominent figure in Colonial historiography, divided Indian history into three distinct periods in his influential work The History of British India. [0370]: * Answer: B. The religion of the dominant rulers (Hindu, Muslim, British)
[0371]: Q2. Nationalist historians like R.C. Majumdar often had complex, nuanced views on major events like the Revolt of 1857. In contrast to the popular nationalist narrative that called it the "First War of Independence," Majumdar famously concluded that it was: A. A highly organized, pan-India national war of independence. B. Neither the first, nor national, nor a war of independence. C. A purely religious conflict orchestrated by the Wahabis. D. A successful agrarian revolution led by peasants.
[0372]: * Answer: B. Neither the first, nor national, nor a war of independence.
[0373]: Q3. The Marxist approach views the national movement as a structured bourgeois movement. Which early Indian communist leader is credited with laying the foundational Marxist critique of the Indian national movement in the 1920s? A. R.P. Dutt B. A.R. Desai C. M.N. Roy D. P.C. Joshi
[0374]: * Answer: C. M.N. Roy
[0375]: Q4. The Subaltern school of historiography emerged in the 1980s. The term "subaltern" itself was originally coined by which prominent Marxist thinker to describe groups marginalized by the hegemony of the ruling classes? A. Karl Marx B. Antonio Gramsci C. Friedrich Engels D. Louis Althusser
